# NECO 36 — Global Basic Needs Dividend

**Status:** Public-interest programme design and feasibility proposal — not a funded or legally authorised universal benefit  
**Version:** 0.1 · 5 November 2026

## 1. Mission

Explore a universal, predictable dividend of **36 NECO per person per day** to help secure food, essential goods and the foundations of a healthy life. The programme must reduce poverty and hunger without turning access to basic needs into a surveillance or behavioural-control system.

The 36 NECO figure is a proposed programme unit, not a claim that NECO currently has a fixed euro value, is legal tender, is redeemable, or is funded. No payment should be promised until the funding, issuer, legal structure, redemption terms and purchasing power are independently established.

## 2. Scale: the arithmetic that must be faced

The 2026 SOFI report estimates 645 million people faced hunger in 2025, equal to 7.8% of the global population. This implies a rough population denominator of 8.27 billion (645m / 0.078); it is an arithmetic approximation, not a population forecast.

At that illustrative population:
- 36 NECO × 8.27 billion = **about 297.7 billion NECO per day**.
- Annualised: **about 108.7 trillion NECO per year**.
- Per person: 1,080 NECO per 30-day month; 13,140 NECO per 365-day year.

These are nominal NECO units. They cannot be converted into euros or dollars until a defensible unit-of-account, price basket, issuer and funding model exist. A unit name alone does not create purchasing power.

Source: FAO / UNICEF / WFP / WHO, SOFI 2026: https://www.fao.org/hunger/en/ and https://data.unicef.org/resources/sofi-2026/

## 3. Proposed benefit design: 36 NECO daily

A rights-based starting allocation for a pilot, to be tested with recipients and local food systems:

| Wallet pocket | Daily allocation | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Everyday essentials | 12 NECO | Flexible essentials: food, household basics, transport, connectivity |
| Food & organic choice | 18 NECO | Food purchases at participating merchants, including organic and agroecological producers |
| Health & participation | 6 NECO | Voluntary healthy activities, community meals, cooking classes, sports, gardens and preventive wellbeing services |
| **Total** | **36 NECO** | |

This split is a pilot hypothesis, not a universal rule. Users must be able to reallocate or opt out of restricted pockets. At minimum, the everyday-essentials amount must remain usable without lifestyle monitoring. No one should lose the basic dividend for choosing conventional food, having a disability, being ill, or not participating in an activity.

## 4. Healthy-food mechanism — enable, don't punish

### Merchant network
Onboard independent grocers, cooperatives, farmers' markets, organic shops, community-supported agriculture (CSA), food hubs, school/community kitchens and local producers.

### Product eligibility
Use transparent, public criteria:
- certified organic products under the relevant jurisdiction's rules can receive an organic incentive;
- fresh or minimally processed staple foods, vegetables, fruit, pulses, whole grains, nuts, eggs and other culturally appropriate nutritious foods qualify;
- local and seasonal products can receive a modest supply-chain incentive where this supports resilience and does not exclude imports needed for food security;
- infant formula, medically required nutrition, disability-related food needs and culturally important staples require accessible exceptions;
- no moral ranking of recipients or foods.

Organic certification is one attribute, not a complete nutrition or sustainability score. Do not claim every organic item is automatically healthy or every non-organic food is unhealthy.

### Incentives
- A merchant-funded or separately funded bonus can increase purchasing power for certified organic and local produce.
- NECO Commons may co-fund community-supported agriculture shares, produce boxes and school/community meals.
- Small merchants receive low-cost QR/POS acceptance and prompt settlement through a regulated PSP.
- Publish merchant fees and prohibit pay-to-rank placement.

## 5. Healthy-habits mechanism — voluntary and privacy-preserving

Offer optional rewards for activities people choose: walking groups, cycling, sports, gardening, cooking workshops, community meals, sleep education, preventive appointments and social connection.

Rules:
- no continuous location tracking, wearable requirement or biometric scoring;
- no penalties for inactivity, illness, disability, pregnancy, age or caregiving;
- no sale of health/activity data and no advertising profiles;
- allow offline paper/community vouchers and human-assisted access;
- reward participation or access costs, not medical outcomes or weight loss;
- keep health records with the health provider, not the NECO wallet.

The best default is to fund access (e.g. a sports club contribution or cooking class) rather than pay people to disclose sensitive health data.

## 6. Distribution and identity

The dividend should be universal in principle, with proportionate safeguards:
- HeavenKey/passkeys are a convenient sign-in option, not proof of legal eligibility.
- Helix ID, BioID and World ID are optional verification methods only where a defined risk justifies them; never require face recognition or proof-of-humanity as the only access route.
- Provide assisted enrolment through trusted local organisations, paper fallback, accessible devices and appeal routes.
- Use the least data necessary. Separate identity proofing from the payment provider's KYC/AML duties.
- Children receive age-appropriate access through a guardian/trustee arrangement with child safeguards, without making the child dependent on a commercial biometric account.
- Protect people without smartphones, bank accounts, fixed addresses or formal identity documents through legally compliant alternatives.

## 7. Funding and issuance architecture

Do not create purchasing power by minting unsupported units. Do not mix a payment token's reserves with social-programme funds.

Potential funding streams to evaluate:
1. public budgets and social-protection transfers;
2. progressive, legally authorised contributions and solidarity levies;
3. philanthropic and development grants;
4. employer / cooperative / merchant contributions;
5. voluntary donations and community endowments;
6. measurable environmental and social procurement programmes, only where additionality and verification are credible.

Each funding stream must be committed, legally available, independently audited and matched to the benefit liability. Carbon credits, token appreciation, speculative trading, uncontracted future revenues and unverified natural-capital valuations must not be counted as cash funding.

### Two-ledger separation
- **Payment / stored-value ledger:** managed by an authorised issuer or payment provider, with safeguarding, redemption and consumer protections as required by law.
- **NECO Commons programme ledger:** records restricted grants, programme commitments, merchant subsidies and impact reporting; it must never be represented as backing a regulated token unless the issuer's legal reserve rules explicitly permit it.

For a daily benefit, funds should be pre-funded or backed by an enforceable public appropriation/contract. Pause new enrolments before creating unfunded payment promises. Protect accrued balances and provide a clear wind-down plan.

## 8. Pilot sequence

### Phase 0 — 0–90 days: design and readiness
- establish an independent, diverse steering group including recipients, food producers, nutrition/public-health experts, disability advocates, privacy experts and payment/legal specialists;
- commission legal analysis for Germany/EU pilot and one partner jurisdiction;
- define the NECO unit's purchasing-power reference basket and whether it is a non-cash voucher, grant entitlement or regulated payment instrument;
- secure committed funds and independent trustee/auditor;
- co-design the product with recipients.

### Phase 1 — 3–6 months: local proof
- 100–500 voluntary participants in one or two communities;
- start with a fully funded grant/voucher programme denominated in euros, optionally displayed as a 36-NECO design equivalent only if the conversion is disclosed and fixed;
- recruit 10–30 food merchants and community organisations;
- test offline access, fraud controls, appeals, accessibility, redemption and merchant settlement;
- compare food affordability, diet diversity, recipient autonomy and local producer revenue against baseline.

### Phase 2 — 6–18 months: multi-region evaluation
- independent evaluation and public financial audit;
- expand only when funding, unit purchasing power, provider capacity and user outcomes are demonstrated;
- test interoperable payment rails through licensed partners;
- publish open protocols and anonymised aggregate impact data.

### Phase 3 — scale decision
Scale only if the programme can demonstrate reliable funding, real purchasing power, inclusive access, low administrative cost, no coercive data collection and measurable improvement in food security.

## 9. Key metrics

- benefit paid on time and value actually redeemed;
- share of participants with improved food security;
- affordability and dietary diversity, not only organic sales;
- participant autonomy and satisfaction;
- local producer and small-merchant revenue;
- share of benefits reaching people excluded from digital banking;
- admin cost per participant;
- fraud/error rates and appeal resolution time;
- privacy incidents and data deletion compliance;
- funded liabilities versus outstanding benefits.

Do not use weight, body composition, biometric identity or location histories as success metrics.

## 10. Governance and safeguards

Establish an independent NECO Commons trustee/foundation with recipient representation, conflict-of-interest rules, published accounts, external audit, complaints and appeal channels. Merchant eligibility must be open, transparent and reviewable. Recipients must be able to spend the flexible portion without permission or judgement.

No token sale, unbacked issuance, guaranteed yield, guaranteed euro conversion or universal-income launch claim is authorised by this proposal.

## 11. Immediate decisions requested

1. Choose the first pilot jurisdiction and community.
2. Select the legal form of the initial benefit (grant, voucher or regulated payment instrument).
3. Secure an independently administered pilot budget.
4. Confirm the unit-of-account and purchasing-power basket.
5. Recruit recipients and merchants to co-design the allocation.
6. Commission legal, tax, safeguarding, privacy and nutrition review.

**NECO principle: dignity first. The mechanism should make good choices easier and affordable — never make food, health or human worth conditional on surveillance.**
